PROOF FIRST
A practical field guide to winning your warranty claim on the record.

WHO THIS IS FOR
Anyone whose phone, television, laptop, or home appliance has failed while still under warranty—and who wants their claim settled by hard evidence and written facts rather than customer-service runarounds.

THE CORE PRINCIPLE
Warranty claims are won on paper. Build your evidence trail early, and require the manufacturer to put every decision in writing.

PHASE 1: BEFORE A FAULT EVER HAPPENS
1. Archive the receipt immediately. Keep the original paper invoice and store a high-resolution scan or PDF in the cloud. Your warranty clock almost always starts on the exact date of purchase.
2. Complete product registration. Log the serial number on the manufacturer's portal right after unboxing; many brands unlock extended coverage when you register within the first 30 days.
3. Audit the warranty terms. Read the fine print once to note the exact coverage period, eligible components, and specific exclusions (such as voltage spikes or liquid contact).
4. Photograph the baseline condition. Snap a few dated photos of the item freshly unboxed and properly installed to prove there was no pre-existing physical damage.

PHASE 2: THE MOMENT SOMETHING BREAKS
5. Capture the malfunction live. Record a crisp photo or short video showing the exact symptom as soon as it occurs, ideally with a timestamp or date visible in the frame.
6. Open a ticket in writing. Submit your claim via email, web form, or the brand's official app rather than relying on phone calls alone, ensuring a permanent paper trail from day one.
7. Log every interaction. Maintain a single running note with every ticket number, date, time, and the name of each representative who handles your case.

PHASE 3: DURING THE TECHNICIAN'S VISIT
8. Secure the service sheet. Never let a technician leave without handing you a signed copy—or digital PDF—of the official visit report or job sheet.
9. Pin down the diagnostic method. Ask the technician to specify which diagnostic tool, test software, or meter reading led to their conclusion, and make sure it is noted on the sheet.
10. Document the inspection yourself. Take clear photos of the opened unit, serial labels, and any internal part the technician points out while they are still on-site.

PHASE 4: IF YOUR CLAIM IS DENIED
11. Demand a written refusal. Ask the company for a formal rejection letter that states the exact technical cause of failure and the specific warranty clause being invoked.
12. Request the lab or inspection report. If the refusal is based on an engineer's assessment, ask for a copy of the underlying technical report and test photos.
13. Escalate to the grievance desk. Locate the manufacturer's formal escalation or appellate contact on their website and send a concise summary referencing your original ticket number.
14. Stick strictly to the timeline and facts. Strip out emotion and present a chronological list of dates, invoices, job sheets, and photos—the exact format a regulator or consumer tribunal expects.
15. File with your consumer protection agency. Every major jurisdiction operates a free consumer ombudsman or dispute portal ready to intervene when a valid warranty is ignored.
